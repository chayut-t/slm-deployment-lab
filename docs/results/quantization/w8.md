## The two candidates

### Why activation width is the axis that matters

Both candidates quantize weights to int8 with the same policy. The only thing
that differs between them, besides the plan row they answer and the cache
requirement they inherit, is the activation width. That is deliberate: it makes
the pair an experiment rather than two guesses.

Weight quantization is the comparatively benign half. A weight tensor is fixed,
known offline, and inspectable before anything runs, so its scale can be chosen
exactly, per output channel, with the error bounded by that channel's own
range. Activation quantization is a different problem: the range has to be
*estimated* from a calibration set and then fixed before any real input
arrives, and a prompt outside the calibrated distribution has no obligation to
respect it.

For a decoder-only transformer the tensor that decides the outcome is the
residual stream. Every block reads it and writes back into it, so it is the
model's accumulator, and its per-channel magnitudes are strongly non-uniform:
a small number of channels carry magnitudes far above the typical one, and a
symmetric per-tensor scale is set by whichever of them is largest.

At 16 bits that costs almost nothing. A symmetric per-tensor int16 range has
32,768 positive levels, so covering the full observed range without clipping
gives up roughly one part in ten thousand of resolution — which is why the
conservative candidate specifies a `min_max` range estimator and does not clip
at all. Percentile and MSE estimators are recorded on the candidate as
explicitly rejected, with reasons: at this width they buy resolution the
datapath does not need and pay for it by clipping exactly the outlier channels
the residual stream depends on.

At 8 bits the same choice inverts. A symmetric per-tensor int8 range has 128
positive levels, so one outlier channel sets a scale that collapses the bulk of
the distribution onto a handful of levels. Clipping error and resolution error
now have to be traded against each other explicitly, which is why the
aggressive candidate specifies an MSE-minimizing threshold search: it states
its objective and introduces no hand-chosen constant. A percentile estimator is
kept as the recorded fallback if the MSE search proves unstable on the
attention tensors. `min_max` is rejected outright at this width — it guarantees
no clipping and guarantees that a single outlier destroys the resolution of
every other channel in the tensor.

Each candidate names the tensors it expects to break first. For `w8a16` those
are the residual stream and the pre-softmax attention scores, whose range grows
with sequence length so that a range fitted at S128 does not bound S4096. For
`w8a8` they are the residual stream, the post-softmax attention probabilities
(which live in `[0, 1]` with a long thin tail, so a symmetric int8 quantizer
spends half its range on values that cannot occur and rounds to zero the small
probabilities that carry the long-context contribution), and the SwiGLU product
entering `mlp_down_proj`, which is reduced over the widest axis in the block.

**None of this is a measurement.** No activation histogram exists anywhere in
this repository, on any host. The sensitivity ordering above is an argument
from the architecture, and each candidate says so in its own `boundary` field:
the first observer pass that ever runs is entitled to contradict it.

Nor is 16-bit activation support established. Whether the public Workbench
compiler accepts a 16-bit activation datapath for this graph on the named
targets is unverified here, because no compile job has been submitted for any
Qwen graph, quantized or floating.

### Weights per output channel, activations per tensor

The weight policy is int8, signed, symmetric, zero point 0, granularity
`per_output_channel`, with float32 scales. The activation policy in both
candidates is signed, symmetric, zero point 0, granularity `per_tensor`.

That asymmetry follows from when the scale has to be chosen. A weight matrix is
available offline, so one scale per output feature can be fitted exactly, and
that removes the dominant source of int8 weight error: a per-tensor weight
scale is set by whichever single output channel has the largest weights, and
the channel-wise magnitude spread inside one projection is wide enough that
such a scale throws away most of the range for most channels. The storage cost
is bounded and small — 344,064 scales at four bytes each, 1,376,256 bytes, or
0.18% of the projected candidate weight bytes. An activation scale has to be
committed to before the data exists, and what it is committed to is one tensor.

Symmetric with a zero zero-point is chosen so integer accumulation stays free
of a cross term: with an asymmetric encoding `x = s(q - z)`, the product of two
asymmetric operands expands into correction sums that the accumulator would
have to carry.

One caveat travels with the per-channel policy and is recorded on the
candidate's `weights` block rather than assumed. "Per output channel" means one
scale per output feature of the *logical* `[out, in]` weight. Which ONNX axis
that is in the exported artifact is not asserted anywhere in this repository:
`torch.onnx.export` emits these projections as `MatMul` with a possibly
transposed initializer, and the committed T21 inventory records node counts,
not per-initializer axis order. Read the axis off the artifact at quantization
time.

### What the exclusion policy protects, and what it costs

Everything not listed in `excluded_from_quantization` is quantized to the
declared precisions. Eight entries are listed, and each is tagged as one of two
kinds, because the two are revised differently:

**Frozen-graph constraints (3).** `tied_embedding_table`,
`final_logits_projection`, and `kv_cache_read_write`. Reversing one of these
means changing the model contract, the T12 graph contract, or the T20/T23
export boundary — work that belongs to other tasks.

**Policy choices (5).** `rmsnorm_scales`, `qwen3_per_head_qk_norm`, `softmax`,
`rope_sin_cos`, and `residual_adds`. T41 made these and T41 may revise them on
evidence.

The policy choices are cheap to keep and expensive to get wrong. The RMSNorm
scale vectors multiply the normalized residual channel by channel before every
matmul, so an int8 rounding error there is a multiplicative error applied to
the accumulator at every one of the 28 blocks. Qwen3's per-head `q_norm` and
`k_norm` vectors act directly on the tensors whose dot product becomes the
attention score. The RoPE sine and cosine tables are exact trigonometric
constants whose quantization error would grow with position index — precisely
the regime the S4096 workload exercises hardest — and the T21 inventory shows
one `Cos` and one `Sin` node, so they are computed once. The residual adds are
the accumulator itself; keeping them wide is what stops activation error from
compounding along depth. The softmax input is unbounded below and its output is
a distribution whose small entries are what long-context attention is made of.

Together, the three excluded *weight* classes that are not the vocabulary table
— the block RMSNorm scales, the per-head q/k norms, and the final norm — total
65,536 parameters. That is 0.011% of the model. The protection is nearly free.

The tied vocabulary table is the opposite. `configs/models/qwen3-0.6b.yaml`
sets `tie_word_embeddings: true`, so the embedding lookup and the final logits
projection are **one stored tensor**. They cannot be given two different
precisions without untying them, which changes the model contract and the
exported graph. The table is 151,936 x 1,024 = 155,582,464 parameters: 26.10%
of the model, and 99.96% of everything the exclusion policy removes from
quantization. The T21 inventory also records the lookup as a `Gather` rather
than a `MatMul`, so the two uses are not even the same operator class, and a
quantizer that handles projection weights would have to handle this table as a
separate case regardless.

The cost is stated rather than hidden. Under this exclusion policy the
projection's weight-byte ratio is 1.585876x. Including the tied table would
take the same arithmetic to about 2.0x — that figure is arithmetic over the
projection's own rows, not a proposal and not a measurement, and it is here so
that the price of the exclusion is legible.

`derive_weight_classes` refuses an untied model contract outright rather than
miscounting it: if `tie_word_embeddings` ever becomes false, the projection
raises instead of quietly counting the vocabulary once.

## Specified, simulated, deployed

The plan's rule for section 7.2 is one sentence: *do not report a simulated
precision as deployed without artifact and compiler evidence.* T41 implements
that as a three-value ladder computed by `assess_precision_state`, which takes
evidence and returns a state together with the scope that must travel with it.

| State | Scope string | What it requires |
|---|---|---|
| `specified` | `candidate_specification_only_no_weight_was_quantized` | nothing; it is the absence of evidence |
| `simulated` | `host_simulation_only_not_compiled_or_executed_on_a_device` | one simulation record |
| `deployed` | `compiled_inferred_and_profiled_through_the_public_ai_hub_pipeline` | the simulation record *and* three chained stage manifests |

Both candidates are `specified` today, and the committed readiness record shows
exactly why: `precision_evidence.source` reads `absent_at_this_commit` and ten
checks are unsatisfied for each candidate.

A `simulated` state needs a record that names the tool, that tool's **exact**
version (a floating tag or a range is refused), the host it ran on, and the
lowercase sha256 of the quantized artifact the simulation produced. Any one of
those missing leaves the candidate at `specified`.

### The digest chain

`deployed` needs everything `simulated` needs, plus all three sanitized AI Hub
stage manifests — compile, inference, and profile — each of which must be a
schema-v2 `ai_hub` manifest, declare the stage it is being offered as evidence
for, and report `status: success`. That is the easy half. The half that makes
the state mean something is the chain:

1. The compile manifest must record the sha256 of the artifact it produced.
2. One of the compile manifest's source-artifact digests must equal the
   quantized-artifact digest the *simulation record* declared. Without that
   link the compiled artifact is not this candidate's artifact.
3. The inference and profile manifests must each cite a
   `predecessor_manifest_sha256` equal to the digest of the compile manifest
   supplied in the same evidence set — **recomputed** here, byte for byte, the
   way `ai_hub.write_manifest` computes it, rather than read from a field.
4. The inference and profile manifests must each consume a `compiled_model`
   source artifact whose sha256 equals the compile manifest's target artifact
   digest.

Compile alone is not `deployed`. Compile plus inference without profile is not
`deployed`. A manifest for the wrong stage, a failed stage, a schema-v1
manifest, a broken predecessor link, and a compile job whose source is some
other artifact are each independently sufficient to keep the state below
`deployed`. Every one of those cases is a test in
`tests/quantization/test_w8_evidence.py`.

### Why the chain, and not the label

Because a label is written by whoever wants the answer, and a digest is not.

`assess_precision_state` reads its input **positively**: it looks for a
simulation record and three chained manifests, and it never consults a `state`,
`precision_state`, `verdict`, or `deployed` key planted anywhere in the input.
A record cannot assert its own conclusion; a test plants exactly that and
checks that the state stays where the evidence puts it.

The recomputation in step 3 is the same idea one level down. If the function
trusted a supplied `predecessor_manifest_sha256`, a caller could hand over a
compile manifest and a digest that do not correspond and the chain would still
appear closed. Recomputing it means the two artifacts have to be the same
artifact.

Two consequences are worth stating plainly. First, the committed candidate
files may only ever read `precision_state: specified` — validation rejects any
other value, because a simulated or deployed state is *evidence*, and evidence
does not live in a config file. Second, unlike T40's parity verdict, `deployed`
here is genuinely reachable from data: a complete, correctly chained evidence
set returns it, and a test proves that too. That is deliberate. The promotion
path is meant to be an auditable set of manifests, not a code edit. What no
input can do is reach `deployed` without the artifacts, and this repository
contains none of them at this commit.

## The KV-cache contract collision

The aggressive candidate cannot satisfy its own plan row, and this is the most
substantive finding T41 produced.

Plan row Q2 asks for an INT8 cache, or a supported type. The cache dtype is not
a knob T41 owns. `slm_lab.contracts.static_cache.CACHE_DTYPE` is frozen at
`float16`, and that dtype is not an internal representation: it is the declared
dtype of 56 `key_cache.N` / `value_cache.N` outputs on every exported prefill
graph, and of 56 cache inputs plus 56 `present_key.N` / `present_value.N`
outputs on every decode graph, and all four committed manifests under
`results/manifests/onnx/` assert it.

So `w8a8` specifies a `float16` cache and records the mismatch in a
machine-readable field — `satisfied_without_contract_change: false` — with
`request_status: out_of_scope_change_request_for_T12_T20_T23`. The alternative,
writing `int8` into a configuration file because a table row asked for it,
would have produced a specification that no exported graph in this repository
can carry.

Lowering the cache dtype would change, in the candidate's own change-control
block:

- the frozen T12 static-cache contract (`CACHE_DTYPE`), owned by **T12**;
- the T20 exported prefill cache outputs and decode cache inputs/outputs,
  owned by **T20**;
- the T23 promoted prefill export and its committed manifests, owned by
  **T23**;
- `results/manifests/onnx/S*.json`, which assert float16 on every cache tensor.

`w8a16` has no such problem: row Q1 admits an FP16 cache outright, so it is
fully specifiable against the frozen contract as it stands, and it records
`request_status: not_raised_at_this_commit`.

The practical consequence outlives the paperwork. A future W8A8 result is not
"Q2 as the plan specifies it"; it is Q2 with a float16 cache, and it must be
reported that way. At S4096 the cache alone is 484,442,112 bytes — 41% of the
float16 weight bytes and 64% of the projected candidate weight bytes — and
weight quantization does not touch it. A weight-only W8 result must never be
presented as a memory result at long context.

## The weight-storage projection

`weight_storage_projection` is arithmetic. Every row it emits carries
`"measurement": "analytic_projection"`, and the mapping carries its own
`does_not_establish` list. It is derived from eight fields of
`configs/models/qwen3-0.6b.yaml` — `num_hidden_layers`, `hidden_size`,
`intermediate_size`, `num_attention_heads`, `num_key_value_heads`, `head_dim`,
`vocab_size`, `tie_word_embeddings` — and from the candidate's own exclusion
policy. Nothing was weighed, timed, or read off a quantized artifact, because
no quantized artifact exists.

| Quantity | Value | Kind |
|---|---:|---|
| Total parameters | 596,049,920 | derived from the model contract |
| Quantized parameters | 440,401,920 (73.89%) | analytic projection |
| Excluded parameters | 155,648,000 (26.11%) | analytic projection |
| float16 weight bytes | 1,192,099,840 | analytic projection |
| Candidate weight bytes | 751,697,920 | analytic projection |
| Weight bytes saved | 440,401,920 | analytic projection |
| Weight-byte ratio | 1.585876x | analytic projection |
| Per-output-channel scales | 344,064 (float32, 1,376,256 bytes) | analytic lower bound |
| Ratio including those scales | 1.582978x | analytic lower bound |

Both candidates produce identical projections, because they share a weight
policy and an exclusion policy; only their activation and cache blocks differ.

The parameter total is cross-checked against the committed artifact rather than
taken on trust, and this is the one place the projection touches a real file's
byte count. The exported float16 prefill graph splits its initializers: 254 go
to external data and 56 stay inline in the protobuf. Those 56 are exactly
`2 x num_hidden_layers`, and the largest of them is 256 bytes, exactly
`head_dim x 2` — the Qwen3 per-head `q_norm` and `k_norm` vectors, which sit
below the exporter's external-data threshold. The committed external data is
1,192,085,504 bytes, which is 596,042,752 float16 elements; adding the 56
inline vectors at 128 elements each gives 7,168 more, for 596,049,920. That
equals the total derived from the model contract, so three independent
committed sources agree, and the cross-check raises rather than warns if they
ever stop agreeing. It is labelled `arithmetic_over_committed_inputs`, not a
measurement of anything quantized; it is there because a parameter count that
disagreed with the artifact would silently poison every ratio above.

**What the projection establishes.** An arithmetic ceiling on weight-byte
reduction under this exclusion policy — about 1.59x, and no better, however
well the quantizer performs — together with an exact statement of what the
exclusion policy costs, since the tied table alone accounts for 155,582,464 of
the 596,049,920 parameters that stay at float16.

**What it does not establish**, from its own list:

- on-disk quantized artifact size;
- encoding, scale, or zero-point storage overhead in the shipped format;
- runtime peak memory;
- any latency or throughput claim;
- KV-cache memory, which both candidates leave at the frozen float16 contract;
- the quality impact of any included or excluded weight class.

The scale figure is a deliberate lower bound and is kept out of the headline
ratio. It counts one float32 per output channel and nothing else: no zero
points (the policy is symmetric, so there are none), no QDQ node overhead, no
per-tensor activation encodings, no container framing. The shipped format is
not decided at this commit, so the real overhead is unknown rather than
estimated.

For the same reason, the projection carries the float16 KV-cache bytes at each
frozen context — 18,350,080 at S128, 66,060,288 at S512, 132,120,576 at S1024,
and 484,442,112 at S4096, computed from the frozen capacities 160, 576, 1,152,
and 4,224 — so that a reader can see which memory term W8 does *not* reduce
without having to go and derive it.

## The quality-delta protocol

`compare_quality` is implemented, enforced, and has nothing to compare. That is
not a euphemism: this repository contains no W8 quality result at this commit,
and `validate_repository` fails if one appears, on the grounds that a record
matching `t41-w8-quality-*.json` is either a fabrication or a real measurement
whose provenance the gate has not been taught to check — both reasons to stop
rather than to trust it.

What the function enforces when a record finally exists:

- **Both sides validate against the frozen T13 result schema.** Not the
  candidate side only. A baseline that does not satisfy the contract is not a
  baseline.
- **Both sides cite this repository's current protocol digest.** A delta
  computed under any other digest is not comparable with one computed under
  `2541fa76…`, and the function refuses to subtract them rather than noting it.
- **Both sides measure the same thing.** Workload, metric, unit, kind, harness
  release and commit, resolved task digest, dataset id, revision, config,
  split, selection, prompt interface, chat-template setting, few-shot count,
  and the actual prompt and generated token counts must all match. Any
  difference is named and the subtraction is refused.
- **The baseline is floating.** `float16`, `bfloat16`, or `float32`; a
  quantized baseline is rejected.
- **The candidate declares its own precision state, and the scope is derived
  from that declaration.** `comparison_scope` is `simulated_vs_float` or
  `deployed_vs_float`, taken from the candidate record and from nothing else.
  There is deliberately no caller argument that could label a simulated
  comparison as deployed, and a test asserts the function signature has none.
- **The declared state is cross-checked against the evidence level.** A
  `deployed` candidate must report `observed_real_device` or
  `observed_hosted_device`; otherwise it must report `simulated`.

The precision state rides on `source.precision` as
`<candidate_id>+<simulated|deployed>` — for example `w8a16+simulated` — because
the frozen T13 result schema sets `additionalProperties: false` at the record
root and inside `source`, so a W8 result has nowhere to add a bespoke
`precision_state` field. Widening that schema would mean changing a frozen T13
contract T41 does not own. The state is instead carried by a field the schema
already requires and cross-checked against one the schema already constrains.

The delta itself is candidate median minus baseline median, in the metric's own
unit. The function does not decide whether a positive delta is good: perplexity
down is better, accuracy up is better, and the reader is told that rather than
guessed at.

### The inherited calibration bias

Every comparison carries the T40 bias forward, read from
`configs/quantization/calibration.yaml` at comparison time rather than
transcribed, so a consumer who reads only the deltas cannot drop the caveat:

- the corpus is 6,912 calibration token positions in total;
- 83.33% of that budget comes from one repeated authored seed, since the four
  context workloads are nested token-ID prefixes of the same T10 sample rather
  than four independent bodies of text;
- four of the thirteen tier-1 samples are both calibrated on and evaluated on.

The direction of the bias is stated with it: the range observers are fitted on
prompts the delta is measured on, so any measured degradation is a *lower
bound* on what an unseen prompt would show. T41 deliberately did not widen the
corpus. T40 left tier-2 candidates declared but uncommitted, and changing the
corpus and introducing the first quantization policy in the same task would
leave any future delta unattributable to either change.

Both previously rested on `user_authorization:qai_hub_submission`. That label
is now discharged, and neither row became reachable: a submission needs an
input artifact, and there is none, so each now names the missing quantize-stage
adapter and the missing AIMET host instead — the two routes that could
produce one. Discharging an authorization does not produce an artifact.

The last `blocked` row in the table, *End-to-end generation impact*, did not
move, and the discharge does not read across it. It carries neither of those
two blockers. It names `upstream_task:T32`, `upstream_task:T33`, and
`user_authorization:device_cloud_session`, and that third string is a
still-live authorization lock of a different kind: an interactive Device Cloud
lease, which is a separate lock from job submission. The readiness record
reads `not cleared here` on it, and the 120-minute ceiling below is the bound
that would apply to such a lease rather than a lease that was taken. One
authorization cleared; this one did not.

### The three blockers, and why they are recorded separately

**B1 — `hardware:linux_cuda_aimet_host`.** AIMET publishes Linux x86-64 wheels
only; the compiled `aimet-onnx` extension has no macOS build. It is pinned at
2.36.0 in `environments/linux-aimet/` and has never been installed or executed
for this repository. The primary macOS host additionally carries no `torch`,
`onnx`, `onnxruntime`, `numpy`, or `transformers` — verified on this host while
writing this report. Lane B cannot run here.

**B2 — `user_authorization:qai_hub_submission`: CLEARED.** On 2026-08-03 the
user authorized AI Hub submission for T41 — hosted compile, profile, and
inference jobs, plus Device Cloud interactive minutes capped at 120, free
capacity only, with no spend permitted without a fresh decision. `AGENTS.md`
requires exactly this before any external job, and it is now on record.

Clearing B2 did not make the acceptance criterion reachable, and this is the
point most likely to be misread later. A submission needs something to submit,
and no W8 artifact exists anywhere in this repository. Producing one takes one
of exactly two routes, and both are shut. Lane B is blocked by B1. Lane A would
need a hosted quantize job, and this repository has no adapter that can request
one: `slm_lab.deployment.qualcomm.ai_hub` declares
`STAGES = {"compile", "inference", "profile"}` and nothing else, and that module
sits under `src/slm_lab/deployment/qualcomm/`, an owned path of **T22**
(`ai/tasks/definitions/T22.yaml`), which is being worked concurrently on
`task/T22-qnn-candidates`. T41 must not add a stage to another task's module
while that task is open. So the blocker that survives the authorization is a
capability and ownership gap, not a permission one, and it is recorded as
`capability:no_quantize_stage_adapter_in_this_repository` with T22 named. The
service itself does expose a quantize entry point — see "What the service
exposes" below — which is what makes this an adapter gap rather than a
platform limitation.

T41 holds the `qai_hub_submission` resource lock so that no other writer
submits a Workbench job under a competing precision policy while this task is
open. It has still not exercised it: no job of any stage was submitted.

**B3 — `upstream_task:T31` (and `T33`).** No Qwen graph of any precision has
traversed the public Workbench pipeline. `T31` ("Qwen Workbench results on
three Qualcomm targets") and `T33` (the integrated floating milestone) are both
`planned` in `ai/tasks/task_graph.yaml`. This is the evidence-interpretability
reason that submitting now, even with authorization in hand and even with an
artifact, would not establish what the acceptance criterion wants: a quantize
or compile failure on an untried graph cannot be attributed to the W8 policy
rather than to the graph, and a success would not isolate either. The criterion
therefore waits on work T41 does not own, independently of B1 and B2.

They are recorded separately because they clear separately and in a specific
order. B1 needs a machine, B3 needs two upstream tasks, and B2 needed a person
and now has one. Merging them into one "no hardware" note would hide that the
authorization is already spent as a decision while the criterion is untouched,
and would invite the mistake of submitting a quantized job before a floating
one has ever succeeded.

### Two capability gaps, recorded where they were found

`slm_lab.deployment.qualcomm.ai_hub` implements compile, inference, and profile
stages and **no quantize stage**. The compile stage consumes a quantized
artifact; it does not create one. So Lane A cannot by itself produce the
quantized artifact at this commit. That gap was recorded separately from the
authorization blocker from the start, and separating them is what makes the
present state legible: the authorization cleared and the gap did not, which
would have been invisible had the two been folded together. The module belongs
to T22, so closing the gap is T22's change to make or T22's to hand over, not
T41's to take.

The two lanes would also not produce the same artifact. Committed T02 access
evidence (`docs/results/access/2026-07-24-public-platform-access.md`) records
the Workbench Quantize Job running AIMET `2.34`, per release notes dated
2026-07-06, while `environments/linux-aimet/` pins `aimet-onnx` and
`aimet-torch` at `2.36.0`. A Lane A artifact and a Lane B artifact are
therefore different artifacts, and their encodings must not be compared as
though a difference between them were a property of the model.

## What the service exposes

This is the one section of this report that touches the live service, and it
is deliberately fenced off from everything above it.

### The authorization, as an event

On 2026-08-03 the user authorized Qualcomm AI Hub submission for T41. The terms
are recorded here in full because `AGENTS.md` requires an external-job record
and because the ceiling has to stay auditable:

| Term | Value |
|---|---|
| Date authorized | 2026-08-03 |
| Scope | Hosted AI Hub compile, profile, and inference jobs for T41 |
| Device Cloud | Interactive minutes permitted, capped at 120 |
| Capacity | Free capacity only |
| Spend | None permitted; any cost needs a fresh decision |
| Still withheld | Pushing, and any public GitHub state |
| Device minutes consumed | 0 of 120 |
| Cost incurred | US$0.00 |
| Jobs submitted | 0 |

The 120-minute ceiling is **fully unspent**. Nothing in this task drew against
it, and a future session inherits the whole allowance.

### The read-only capability query

Acting on that authorization, one read-only capability query was run against
the live service with the pinned client `qai-hub==0.53.0` on 2026-08-03. It
enumerated what the API offers and which devices are available. It submitted no
job, reserved no device, consumed no Device Cloud minutes, and cost nothing.

It is recorded at `results/quantization/t41-ai-hub-capability-2026-08-03.json`.
The live query is:

```bash
uv run python -m slm_lab.quantization.w8 capabilities
```

It needs an authenticated `qai-hub` client at the recorded version, and this
repository pins no Qualcomm client in `pyproject.toml`, so the live command
does not run on the primary host. It ran in an environment that carried one and
the sanitized result was carried in. The committed record is its own saved
query and rebuilds without a network:

```bash
uv run python -m slm_lab.quantization.w8 capabilities \
  --offline-input results/quantization/t41-ai-hub-capability-2026-08-03.json
```

`check` recomputes the record on every run and fails if the two disagree.

The query called exactly one service function, `get_devices`, which returns
public device metadata. No `submit_*` function was called, and none was even
fetched through the descriptor protocol: the entry-point names come from
`dir()` and the signature from `inspect.getattr_static`, which reads `__dict__`
directly. No job was created, no model was uploaded, and no device was leased.

Three things were observed.

**A quantize entry point exists in the client.** The client exposes
`submit_quantize_job(model, calibration_data, weights_dtype, activations_dtype,
name, options, project)`, and `QuantizeDtype` carries the members `INT4`,
`INT8`, and `INT16`. Both T41 candidates are therefore expressible as public
quantize requests without inventing anything: `w8a16` maps to INT8 weights with
INT16 activations, `w8a8` to INT8 with INT8. `INT4` exists too, which matters
to T42 rather than here. This is what turns "Lane A cannot produce the
artifact" from a platform limitation into an adapter gap: the service takes
the request, and this repository has nothing that sends it.

**All three plan section 3.2 target devices are live.** 79 devices were
enumerated, including the primary and both comparison targets the candidates
already name:

| Candidate selector | os | Hexagon | Advertised |
|---|---|---|---|
| `Snapdragon X Elite CRD` | 11 | v73 | `framework:qnn`, `htp-supports-fp16:true` |
| `Snapdragon 8 Elite QRD` | 15 | v79 | `framework:qnn`, `htp-supports-fp16:true` |
| `Dragonwing IQ-9075 EVK` | 1.7 | v73 | `framework:qnn`, `htp-supports-fp16:true` |

**The quantize API has no separate KV-cache dtype knob.** The parameter list
above is the whole surface: weights and activations, and nothing that addresses
the cache. That is consistent with the frozen `CACHE_DTYPE = "float16"` finding
recorded above and is emphatically not an escape from it — the hosted path
offers no way to request an INT8 cache either, so `w8a8` remains Q2 with a
float16 cache on both lanes.

### What this does not establish

The query read an API surface and a device list on one date. It says nothing
about this model. Specifically it does not establish:

- that the Qwen graph, floating or quantized, compiles for any of those
  devices;
- that a 16-bit activation datapath is accepted for this graph;
- any operator support, NPU placement, or fallback behaviour;
- any latency, throughput, memory, or energy figure;
- any accuracy or quality result;
- that a quantize job for either candidate would succeed, or what its
  encodings would contain.

**No job was submitted and no W8 candidate traversed the public pipeline.** The
acceptance criterion is not satisfied and this query does not move it. A
capability query is not a pipeline traversal, and the `htp-supports-fp16`
attribute in the table above is a device advertisement, not a result.

## How to close each blocker

The first question is no longer "may we submit?" — that is answered — but
"what is there to submit?". So the ordered next action is to obtain a W8
artifact, by one of the two routes that can produce one, and only then to spend
the authorization. Every command printed here either exists at this commit or
is marked as not existing. Placeholders in angle brackets are private,
machine-local paths.

### Step 0 — choose a route to a W8 artifact

Nothing downstream can start without one, and there are exactly two routes.

- **Route A, hosted quantize.** The service accepts it: the client exposes
  `submit_quantize_job` with `QuantizeDtype.INT8` and `QuantizeDtype.INT16`,
  which covers both candidates. This repository cannot send it.
  `slm_lab.deployment.qualcomm.ai_hub` has no quantize stage, and the module is
  an owned path of **T22** (`ai/tasks/definitions/T22.yaml`), worked
  concurrently on `task/T22-qnn-candidates`. Closing this route means T22 adds
  a quantize adapter, or hands the module over. It is not a change T41 may make
  while T22 is open, and it is not an authorization problem.
- **Route B, local AIMET.** Blocked on B1 below. Independent of T22 and of the
  service entirely.

Route B is the one a future T41 session can start unilaterally; Route A needs
a cross-task decision first. Whichever is taken, the artifact it produces is
the input every step below assumes.

### B1 — provision the Linux + CUDA AIMET host

1. Provision a Linux x86-64 host with glibc >= 2.34, an NVIDIA GPU of compute
   capability >= 5.2 and driver >= 455, per
   `environments/linux-aimet/README.md`.
2. Build the pinned environment from the committed lock files, at the commit
   that owns them:

   ```bash
   uv python install 3.11.13
   uv venv --python 3.11.13 .ai-local/envs/t40-aimet
   export AIMET_VENV="$PWD/.ai-local/envs/t40-aimet"

   uv pip install --python "${AIMET_VENV}/bin/python" \
     --require-hashes -r environments/linux-aimet/aimet-requirements.lock

   uv pip install --python "${AIMET_VENV}/bin/python" --no-deps \
     --require-hashes -r environments/linux-aimet/aimet-cuda-wheels.lock
   uv pip install --python "${AIMET_VENV}/bin/python" onnxruntime-gpu
   ```

   The second install is a recorded divergence from the lock: it swaps in the
   `+cu126` AIMET distributions and adds a package that is not hash-pinned.
   Record whether it was applied and which AIMET distribution the host ended up
   with. The PyPI distribution and the GitHub `+cu126` distribution share the
   release number `2.36.0` and declare different `torch` majors, so the release
   number alone does not identify the build.
3. Fill `environments/linux-aimet/aimet-host.template.json` into a real host
   manifest and validate it, remembering that the validator is not a
   completeness gate:

   ```bash
   PYTHONPATH=src python -m slm_lab.manifests.cli host \
     <filled-host-manifest>.json
   ```

4. Close the numerical half of T40's parity gate *before* publishing any delta.
   It is recorded `not_run` in
   `results/quantization/t40-baseline-parity-2026-08-02.json` and is owned by
   T21. A quality delta whose floating side has never been verified against the
   PyTorch reference is not a quality delta.

   ```bash
   SLM_LAB_ARTIFACT_ROOT=<external-root> PYTHONPATH=src \
     python -m slm_lab.quantization.parity verify
   SLM_LAB_ARTIFACT_ROOT=<external-root> PYTHONPATH=src \
     <python-with-torch-and-onnxruntime> -m pytest -q tests/onnx
   ```

5. Write the Lane B runner. **It does not exist at this commit.** The readiness
   ledger records its intended shape — a module taking `--candidate` and
   `--calibration configs/quantization/calibration.yaml` — under the command
   status `not_implemented_at_this_commit`, and that placeholder should not be
   printed anywhere as though it were a command. Run `w8a16` first, then
   `w8a8`: the conservative candidate isolates weight error, and a W8A8 result
   is only interpretable next to it.
6. Record the simulation evidence at
   `results/quantization/t41-<candidate_id>-precision-evidence.json` with a
   `simulation` block naming `tool`, an exact `tool_version`, `host`, and
   `quantized_artifact_sha256`. Then:

   ```bash
   uv run python -m slm_lab.quantization.w8 status
   ```

   will report `precision_state=simulated` for that candidate, computed from
   the record rather than asserted by it.
7. Produce the quality result under the frozen T13 evaluation, with
   `source.precision` set to `<candidate_id>+simulated` and
   `system.evidence_level` set to `simulated`, and compare:

   ```bash
   uv run python -m slm_lab.quantization.w8 compare \
     --baseline <float16-result.json> --candidate <w8-result.json>
   ```

### B3 — establish that the floating graph traverses the pipeline

8. T31 must show a floating Qwen graph compiling, running inference, and
   profiling on a public target; T33 integrates that milestone. Neither is
   owned by T41. If Qwen is blocked there, plan section 3.4 applies: isolate

   the smallest verified fallback model — labelling the resulting W8 evidence
   as fallback evidence and never as Qwen evidence.

### B2 — submit under the standing authorization

9. **Confirm the authorization still covers what you are about to do.** The
   2026-08-03 grant covers hosted compile, profile, and inference for T41, plus
   Device Cloud interactive minutes capped at 120, on free capacity only, with
   zero spend. It does not cover a paid job, a device outside free capacity, or
   the 121st minute — each of those needs a fresh decision. Nothing was drawn
   against the ceiling, so all 120 minutes remain. Hold the
   `qai_hub_submission` resource lock for the duration. The device policy in

   Dragonwing IQ-9075 EVK and Snapdragon 8 Elite QRD as comparisons; the
   capability query confirmed all three are live, and the candidates carry
   those selectors with the full attribute vocabulary the query observed.
   Generation re-checks every selector against
   `results/quantization/t41-ai-hub-capability-2026-08-03.json` and fails if
   they drift, so those lists are deliberately over-constrained: if a selector
   stops resolving, trim it to the device name before concluding the device is
   gone.
10. Compose the compile request from the committed candidate plus private
    paths. This writes a file and submits nothing:

    ```bash
    uv run python -m slm_lab.quantization.w8 request \
      --candidate w8a16 --stage compile --context 128 --graph prefill \
      --quantized-artifact <private-path>.onnx \
      --output-artifact <private-path>.serialized.bin \
      --request-out .ai-local/profiles/T41/compile-request.json
    ```

11. Submit the three stages as separate processes, in order, through the T30
    adapters:

    ```bash
    PYTHONPATH=src python3 scripts/qualcomm/compile.py \
      --request .ai-local/profiles/T41/compile-request.json \
      --manifest results/processed/qualcomm/t41-compile-manifest.json
    PYTHONPATH=src python3 scripts/qualcomm/inference.py \
      --request .ai-local/profiles/T41/inference-request.json \
      --manifest results/processed/qualcomm/t41-inference-manifest.json
    PYTHONPATH=src python3 scripts/qualcomm/profile.py \
      --request .ai-local/profiles/T41/profile-request.json \
      --manifest results/processed/qualcomm/t41-profile-manifest.json
    ```

    The inference and profile requests are emitted by the same `w8 request`
    command with `--stage inference` or `--stage profile`, plus
    `--predecessor-manifest` pointing at the sanitized compile manifest and
    `--compiled-artifact` pointing at the downloaded compiled model;
    `--stage inference` additionally requires `--input-dataset`.
12. Only once all three sanitized manifests exist may a candidate move to
    `deployed`, and only by adding them to that candidate's precision-evidence
    record, where `assess_precision_state` will verify the chain. Then update
    `ai/tasks/task_graph.yaml`, regenerate with
    `uv run python scripts/ai/render_task_status.py`, and publish the worklog.

Request files are never committed: they carry machine-local paths, as
`scripts/qualcomm/README.md` states. The emitter enforces this rather than
trusting it — a request or output path inside the repository that is not under
`.ai-local/` or `artifacts/` is refused.

## Reproducing this report

Everything below was run from the repository root at this commit, on macOS with
no quantization stack installed. Every command in this section is offline
except the one explicitly marked otherwise.

```bash
uv run python -m slm_lab.quantization.w8 check     # full offline validation
uv run python -m slm_lab.quantization.w8 status    # precision state + ledger
uv run python -m slm_lab.quantization.w8 generate  # rewrite both candidates
uv run python -m slm_lab.quantization.w8 check     # still passes: unchanged
uv run --extra dev python -m pytest tests/quantization -q
```

`check` exits 0 and prints `T41 W8 candidate check passed: <checkout path>`,
where the trailing field is this machine's absolute checkout path and is not
reproduced here. It re-renders both documents from committed inputs and
compares them to the files on disk byte for byte, so running `generate` and
then `check` is the fixed-point proof; once `configs/quantization/w8/` is
tracked, `git diff -- configs/quantization/w8` after a `generate` is empty as
well. The test run reports **225 passed, 1 skipped**, of which 132 are the T41
tests in
`tests/quantization/test_w8_candidates.py` and
`tests/quantization/test_w8_evidence.py`. The wider suite,
`uv run --extra dev python -m pytest tests -q`, reports 787 passed and 15
skipped.

The one command in this report that contacts the service is the capability
query, `uv run python -m slm_lab.quantization.w8 capabilities`. It reads and
does not write, but it needs an authenticated `qai-hub` client, which this
repository does not pin, so it does not run on the primary host. The committed
record replays offline:

```bash
uv run python -m slm_lab.quantization.w8 capabilities \
  --offline-input results/quantization/t41-ai-hub-capability-2026-08-03.json
```

The submission boundary can be exercised without any authorization, because it
fails closed on the artifact that does not exist:

```bash
uv run python -m slm_lab.quantization.w8 request \
  --candidate w8a16 --stage compile --context 128 --graph prefill \
  --quantized-artifact <any-nonexistent-path>.onnx \
  --output-artifact .ai-local/profiles/T41/w8a16-S128-prefill.serialized.bin \
  --request-out .ai-local/profiles/T41/compile-request.json
```

It exits 1, writes nothing, and prints:

```text
error: quantized_artifact is missing: <path>. No W8 artifact exists at this
commit: AIMET is Linux + CUDA only and is specified rather than installed, and
this repository's AI Hub adapter implements compile, inference, and profile but
no quantize stage. Produce the artifact on a Lane B host first, then re-run
this emitter.
```

The readiness record is rewritten with:

```bash
uv run python -m slm_lab.quantization.w8 record
```

which validates the whole repository first and then writes
`results/quantization/t41-w8-readiness-<date>.json`. Note two things about the
committed record. Its `generated_at` is the wall-clock time of the run that
wrote it and is not reproduced here, because re-running `record` on the same
day rewrites the committed file with a new timestamp. And its
`repository.git_tree_clean` is `false`, because the T41 files themselves were
uncommitted when it was written — recorded rather than tidied away, since the
alternative is a record that claims a cleanliness it did not have.

## Evidence boundaries

From the readiness record's own `claim_boundary.does_not_establish`, nothing
here establishes:

- any quantized weight, activation, or encoding;
- simulated or deployed precision for either candidate;
- a quantized quality delta, perplexity, or accuracy figure;
- compiler acceptance, operator support, or NPU placement;
- on-disk quantized artifact size or encoding overhead;
- latency, throughput, peak memory, or energy;
- that the floating Qwen graph traverses the public pipeline at all.

What it does establish, and only this:

- two frozen, regenerable W8 candidate specifications bound to committed
  inputs;
- an explicit inclusion/exclusion policy that separates T41's choices from the
  frozen graph's constraints;
- an analytic weight-storage projection derived from the model contract and
  reconciled against the committed float16 export;
- a fail-closed precision-state function that cannot reach `deployed` from
  anything this repository contains;
- an AI Hub schema-v2 request emitter that stops at the submission boundary;
- and, from the read-only capability query alone, that on 2026-08-03 the
  `qai-hub==0.53.0` client exposed a quantize entry point covering both
  candidates' dtype pairs, and that all three plan section 3.2 target devices
  were live. That is an API and fleet observation. It is not a result for this
  model, and no job was submitted to obtain it.

The machine-readable release field is named for its scope:
`released_for_submission_preparation_only`. It is `true`, and what it means is
spelled out beside it — both specifications regenerate byte-identically, every
binding still hashes to its recorded value, and the emitter can compose a
request. It does not mean a request was written, a job was submitted, a weight
was quantized, or a precision was achieved.
